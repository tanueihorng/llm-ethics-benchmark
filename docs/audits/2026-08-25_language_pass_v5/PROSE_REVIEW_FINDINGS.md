## Major findings (18)

### Abstract + Chapter 1 (4)
- **[M1]** "To address this gap, this study evaluates" — "Scorer validity is examined with the official HarmBench classifier, an independent API judge, Llama Guard, and human-labelled samples" — the classifier appears as an instrument for examining its own validity (recurs §1.5). → "examined by cross-checking the official HarmBench classifier against an independent API judge, Llama Guard, and human-labelled samples."
- **[M2]** "The primary experiment covers five matched fp16–NF4" — "pairs from four model families: Qwen3-1.7B, …, and Phi-4-mini" — colon promises four families, delivers five models. → "five matched fp16–NF4 pairs — … — drawn from four model families."
- **[M3]** "Scorer-validation study. HarmBench results are cross-checked" — "two human-labelled samples" — reads as n=2 data points. → "two human-annotated gold sets."
- **[M4]** (Abstract, minor-tagged but user-visible) "The statistical framework applied paired bootstrap…" — framework cannot "apply" tests. → "The analysis applied …".

### Chapter 2 (2)
- **[M5]** "Table 2.1 compares the studies relevant" — "compares the studies … rather than a claim that this design is unique" — faulty parallelism (the table compares a claim). → "Table 2.1 positions the studies relevant to this experiment; it does not claim that this design is unique."
- **[M6]** "The three broad trustworthiness suites also illustrate" — "benefits from fewer outcomes whose scoring criteria are defined explicitly" — garbled logic. → "benefits from a small number of outcomes with explicitly defined scoring criteria, because each prompt-level scoring decision can be traced to the reported metric."

### Chapter 3 (2–3)
- **[M7]** "The report avoids a stronger claim" — "avoids a stronger claim of exact historical rerun because not every earliest package version was captured" — missing article, illogical "not every earliest", near-duplicates §3.7 disclosure two paragraphs earlier. → "The report avoids claiming an exact historical rerun, because the package versions of the earliest runs were not captured in the immutable outputs — a limitation already noted above."
- **[M8]** "The project uses automated tests and" — Wilcoxon [60] and power-guidance [61] citations parked in §3.7 (Reproducibility Controls) instead of §3.5's methods paragraph. **Fix constraint:** moving the markers earlier changes IEEE first-use order, so this requires renumbering or rewording-in-place — decide during fix pass.
- (item 3, tagged minor) "configurations created through the dashboard are written outside the primary configuration" — elided head noun. → "…outside the primary configuration directory and never overwrite it."

### Chapter 4 (4)
- **[M9]** §4.1 scope contradiction — "the effects that remain statistically significant concern capability" is contradicted by the next sentence's significant over-refusal decrease. → "Apart from one over-refusal result discussed below, the effects that remain statistically significant concern capability."
- **[M10]** Delta sign convention unstable — "the Phi-4-mini over-refusal reduction of −0.048" (a reduction of −x is self-contradictory) alternating with magnitude forms ("a substantial decrease of 0.090"). **Fix constraint: wording only; no number may change.** → magnitude + direction word ("a decrease of 0.048") throughout prose.
- **[M11]** "Figure 1.1 places all five pairs in" — Chapter 4 cites Figure 1.1 amid 4.x figures; probable stale cross-reference. **Verify the intended target figure before changing.**
- **[M12]** "the independent semantic judge" vs "the API judge" vs "the semantic classifier" — readers cannot tell how many judges exist (= T2).

### Chapter 5 (3)
- **[M13]** §5.2 — "Llama Guard agrees on direction but not on the classifier's uncorrected significance" — broken construction; "Similarly" wrongly equates a direction disagreement with a significance disagreement. → "Similarly, on Llama, Llama Guard reproduces the classifier's direction but not its uncorrected significance."
- **[M14]** §5.4 — "Read together with the other four pairs, which are also non-significant under the judge, this leaves no over-refusal increase detectable under semantic evaluation" — dangling modifier + contorted predicate; also should carry the §5.7 non-establishment caveat. → "Together with the other four pairs, which are also non-significant under the judge, this result indicates that no over-refusal increase is detectable under semantic evaluation at the sample sizes used; it does not establish that no such increase exists (Section 5.7)."
- **[M15]** §5.7 (and §5.8) — four independent clauses after a colon separated only by commas (comma-spliced enumeration). → semicolons or a bulleted list.

### Chapters 6–7 + appendices (5)
- **[M16]** §6.1 — "The results support neither common oversimplification: neither that quantization inherently improves safety alignment, nor that it systematically degrades it." — double "neither" ungrammatical; presupposes unintroduced oversimplifications. → "The results refute both common oversimplifications: that quantization inherently improves safety alignment and that it systematically degrades it."
- **[M17]** §6.2 — "The absence of a corrected increase in this study does not contradict those findings. It narrows the setting to ordinary NF4 and INT8 loading…" — "It" attaches to an antecedent that cannot narrow a setting. → "This study instead narrows the setting to ordinary NF4 and INT8 loading of five unmodified instruction-tuned checkpoints under direct requests."
- **[M18]** §6.2 — "In the IEEE version of record, Table 14 reports safety ratios and defines the difference as FP16 minus quantized, so a positive difference means … the opposite orientation to the attack success rates in the neighbouring rows [10]." — "Table 14" reads as this report's table; 55-word four-claim run-on. → split: "In the IEEE version of record of [10], Table 14 reports safety ratios, defined as FP16 minus quantized. A positive value therefore indicates that the quantized model was judged less safe — the opposite orientation to the attack success rates in the neighbouring rows."
- **[M19]** §6.3 — "…direct-request threat models, and statistical effect boundaries" — conclusions cannot "apply to" effect boundaries. → "…direct-request threat models, and effects within the statistically detectable range."
- **[M20]** §6.3 — "because a compressed model is chosen for deployment, not only for a benchmark" — misplaced "only" inverts the contrast. → "because models are compressed for deployment, not merely to perform well on a benchmark."
