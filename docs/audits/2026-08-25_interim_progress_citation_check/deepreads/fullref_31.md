- **Publication status:** Peer-reviewed **main-track ICML 2024** paper (PMLR volume 235, Vienna), graded directly from the PDF's own proceedings footer (extract 68–70).
- **Problem & context:** Compression has become the default route to resource-efficient LLM inference while SoTA methods preserve benign task performance, so the safety/trust consequences have been "largely neglected" (abstract 15–18); existing evaluations cover either benign utility only or one-two trust dimensions (106–110), motivating the claimed first comprehensive evaluation of compressed LLMs across eight trust dimensions plus utility ("we decode the compressed trust", 117–123).
- **Approach:** Three dense 13B models — LLaMA2 13b, LLaMA2 13b Chat, Vicuna 13b Chat (322–332) — compressed by five training-free/data-free techniques (333–335): **pruning** with Magnitude/SparseGPT/Wanda, all at 2:4 (=50%) semi-structured sparsity for hardware-friendliness (339–344, Table 1 at 217–278), and **weight-only quantization** with GPTQ and AWQ at 3/4/8-bit (calibration sets of 128 samples where used, Table 1). **Knowledge distillation is named in the related-work taxonomy but never evaluated** (150–153, 1200–1204). Evaluation = MMLU (57 tasks, 301–305) + DecodingTrust's eight dimensions: stereotype, privacy, toxicity, fairness, ethics, adversarial robustness (AdvGLUE++), OOD robustness, robustness to adversarial demonstrations (306–309) + refusal rates as a complementary signal (309–320), on a 0–100 "points" scale (449–451). Toxicity is scored by **Perspective API** over four user-prompt groups (non-toxic, toxic, GPT-3.5-targeted, GPT-4-targeted) crossed with benign/adversarial system prompts (3154–3157, 3167–3195). Refusal handling was deliberately modified from original DecodingTrust for high-refusing open-source 13B models: refusals count as "fair failure" in Fairness and as successful immoral-action detection in Ethics (1348–1367). Design comparisons: pre-trained 7b vs 7b-sized models compressed from 13b at 50% (§4, Fig 2); deeper 3/4-bit sweeps on all three models (§5, Figs 3/10/11); GPTQ extension compressing LLaMA2 Chat at 7b/13b/70b (Table 3, 1746–1758); MT-Bench instruction-following probe (Table 4, 1984–2011). On the title's implication: **"Decoding" refers to decoding trustworthiness with the DecodingTrust benchmark, not to generation-decoding parameters** — the extract contains no temperature/top-p/sampling experiment (grep: zero hits), and the closest decoding-behaviour findings are refusal-rate shifts and instruction-formatting failures (see Key findings).
- **Key findings:** (1) **Quantization beats pruning for joint efficiency+trustworthiness**: a 4-bit quantized model retains its original's trustworthiness while 2:4 pruning significantly degrades it even at 50% sparsity (abstract 21–24; recommendation restated at 936–939). 8-bit is uniformly comparable (<3-point drops, 654–655); **4-bit is the recommended operating point for LLaMA2 Chat 13b, keeping every dimension within a 5-point loss** (639–642, 900–903). (2) **Moderate quantization can unexpectedly improve trust dimensions**: Ethics rises 54.1 → 76.3 (GPTQ) or 62.8 (AWQ) at 4-bit (731–736); fairness EOD drops by over 0.2 in 16-shot settings without raising refusals (781–789); gains disappear at 3-bit (806–809) and replicate across all three models at 4-bit (811–816); Pearson analysis confirms GPTQ improves fairness and AWQ improves AdvGLUE++ at lower bits while most other dimensions degrade (1385–1396). (3) **Extreme 3-bit quantization erodes trust invisibly to benign metrics**: both methods raise risks across multiple dimensions with GPTQ worst-case degradations over 50 points — specifically **~50-point toxicity and ~30-point OOD-robustness drops for GPTQ 3-bit, vs a ~10-point fairness worst case for AWQ** (906–928) — while the same models look near-fine on MMLU (919–922; echoed at 1755–1758 where a 7b source loses only 1.6 MMLU but >5 points on OOD/Ethics/Fairness). (4) **The mechanism is behavioural/refusal-mediated**: GPTQ 3-bit answers ~80% of toxic-benchmark prompts versus 10–60% for denser models, effectively ignoring the "no toxic content" system prompt (948–953), mis-formats OOD answers ('neg'/'pos'/empty, 954–957), and collapses on MT-Bench to 2.89 vs 6.55 at 4-bit and 7.00 dense (1998–2011) — the authors attribute 3-bit failures to loss of instruction-following (958–960, 1736–1739). The **toxicity increase under 3-bit quantization is a refusal collapse**, not newly toxic capability per se (3213–3217, 3248–3250). Separately, **both AWQ and GPTQ induce more stereotype bias in the ordinary benign setting** (3207–3211, alert at 3230–3234). (5) **Adversarial angle:** an adversary may deliberately apply GPTQ 3-bit to break alignment at only ~an 8-point MMLU cost (928–930). (6) **Scale matters:** compression is less reliable from smaller sources — the 7b source model suffers >5-point losses where 13b stays within 5 (1749–1758, Table 3 cells 1866–1899), while 70b is largely stable (1831–1864); pre-trained 7b models beat their 13b counterparts on 3–4 trust dimensions (fairness consistently) despite losing MMLU/AdvDemo/Ethics (477–491, 651–653).

- **Limitations & caveats:** All primary sources are 13B-parameter models, with a GPTQ extension to 7b/70b Chat (Table 3) — nothing below 7B source scale is tested, so behaviour in this FYP's 1.7–4B regime is unsampled (my inference, flagged as such). Quantization uses offline PTQ libraries (AutoGPTQ/AWQ, 1343–1346, 1407–1413), not on-the-fly BitsAndBytes NF4 loading, so magnitudes transfer analogically at best (my inference). Pruning is restricted to hardware-friendly 2:4 patterns (339–344), leaving unstructured pruning untested. The refusal-handling modifications to DecodingTrust (1348–1367) aid internal comparability but break strict comparability with original-DecodingTrust results, and many headline movements are mediated by refusal-rate changes rather than content changes (e.g., the 8-bit "fairness gain" comes with >50% refusal rates, 785–789; toxicity worsens when refusal collapses, 3213–3217) — the authors themselves note refused answers can reflect unawareness rather than safety (312–315). Several quantitative claims live in figure graphics whose cell values are only partially serialized in the text layer (Figs 2/3); quoted numbers here come from legible prose/table regions only. High-compression quality varies strongly with the random calibration set (variance up to 15 at GPTQ 3-bit; 969–975, Fig 7), and the extract shows no general significance-testing framework beyond repeated calibration draws and Pearson correlations (1385–1390) — differences are mostly described qualitatively (my inference). Knowledge distillation is discussed but never run (150–153).
- **Relevance to this FYP:** This is the report's designated nearest prior analogue: its 4-bit preservation finding (639–642) is consistent in direction with this FYP's null-safety-at-NF4 result, and the v5 report already cites it exactly that way in §2.4 and §7.7 (with the honest scoping that Hong et al. use larger models and a different trust taxonomy). Its sharpest warning lives at 3 bits (~50-pt toxicity, ~30-pt OOD; 925–928), outside this FYP's NF4/INT8 grid — supporting the FYP's practice of claiming preservation only for the precisions actually tested, not extrapolating downward. Methodologically, the paper's demonstration that refusal-rate shifts manufacture trust-score movements (toxicity rise = refusal collapse; "fairness gain" = fair refusal, 785–789, 3213–3217) independently supports this FYP's separation of refusal-style scorers (XSTest parser/judge) from harmful-compliance scoring (HarmBench classifier) and its distrust of single-axis proxies. Its core blind-spot claim — benign MMLU cannot reveal trust regressions (919–922, 1755–1758) — directly motivates this FYP's matched-pair design measuring capability and safety jointly. Boundaries when citing: toxicity there is Perspective-API-scored free generation within DecodingTrust, not HarmBench/XSTest compliance rates, so comparisons must stay qualitative; and its models (LLaMA2/Vicuna 13B family, offline PTQ) differ from this FYP's Qwen/Llama/Mistral/Phi pairs loaded with on-the-fly NF4.
