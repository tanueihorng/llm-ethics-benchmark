## Upgraded summary

- **Bibliography entry:** [27] I. Proskurina, L. Brun, G. Metzler, and J. Velcin, "When quantization affects confidence of large language models?," in Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: NAACL 2024, pp. 1918–1928, 2024. Author list and title verified against the PDF title block (extract 1–4); the repo bibliography's short venue form "Findings of NAACL, 2024" matches the footer's full proceedings name. The trailing "arXiv:2405.00632" in the repo entry is **not verifiable from this PDF** — the file carries no arXiv stamp anywhere in the extracted text.
- **Repo PDF:** `Sources/[11] Proskurina et al. - When quantization affects confidence of large language models.pdf` (filename carries an earlier `[11]` numbering while the bibliography numbers it [27]; the extracted text shows running page numbers 1918–1928, e.g. extract 94–96 through 2113).
- **Publication status:** Peer-reviewed conference paper in the **Findings track of NAACL 2024** (Association for Computational Linguistics), pages 1918–1928, June 16–21, 2024 — graded honestly from the PDF's own footer (extract 94–96). Findings is the ACL companion track: externally reviewed and archived, but the acceptance bar is below the main research track, so it grades below a main-conference NAACL paper while remaining a legitimate archival venue.
- **Problem & context:** Prior work judged compression mainly by the accuracy gap and often found it negligible, which is precisely why the authors pose the null hypothesis that compressed and full predictive probability distributions are indistinguishable (442–444); they argue less attention has gone to *explaining* compression loss, especially its variance across texts, and claim to be the first to explain quantization loss through predictive probabilities/confidence (82–89, 446–448).
- **Approach:** Post-training quantization with **GPTQ (OPTQ)** to **4 bits**, group size 128, calibrated on a random sample of 128 C4 sequences (511–522; full config in Table 3, 976–1010). Models: BLOOM, OPT, Mistral-7B and LLaMA-7B (641–643), with scale sweeps over BLOOM 560M–7.1B and OPT 125M–13B (644–647); the headline comparisons use ~7B members of each family (Table 2, 612–615). Zero-shot evaluation (§3.2 at 524; also 774) over six commonsense benchmarks — ARC-Easy, BoolQ, PIQA, HellaSwag, OpenBookQA, XStory-EN (631–635) — built on EleutherAI lm-evaluation-harness scripts with Auto-GPTQ on one A100 (1115–1120). **Confidence metrics:** accuracy plus Expected Calibration Error for binary tasks and Adaptive Calibration Error for multi-class (528–537, formulas 1101–1113); mean prediction confidence split into confidence on wrong predictions (Conf_err) and on the true class (Conf_true) (620–625); mean predictive entropy (×100) (612–615); and Jensen-Shannon divergence between full and quantized true-class probability distributions (690–692, 1020–1026). Significance: paired t-test at α = 0.05, marked ⋆ (617–618).
- **Key findings:** (1) GPTQ 4-bit lowers confidence in true labels with effects varying across model families and scales (abstract 64–68; statistically significant true-class confidence drops confirmed at 651–652). (2) Quantization amplifies already-high calibration error across families and benchmarks; the sharpest callout is LLaMA-7B with a ~10% increase in pre-compression calibration error on HellaSwag (708–713) — arithmetic-consistent with Table 1's HellaSwag CE cell for LLaMA-7B (full 37.8, +4.07 after quantization, 160; 4.07/37.8 ≈ 10.8%) — and HellaSwag scores are hit harder than BoolQ/PIQA (714–716). (3) Overconfidence persists before and after compression, mean confidence ~0.95 even on incorrect predictions (717–722; Table 2 Conf_err ≈ 95.0–95.7 on the ×100 table scale, 558–559, 573–574, 588–589, 603–604). (4) On HellaSwag at ~7B, true-class confidence drops significantly for all four families — BLOOM 46.24→45.23⋆, OPT 50.37→49.78⋆, Mistral 61.14→59.73⋆, LLaMA 56.83→53.69⋆ (×100; 561–562, 576–577, 591–592, 606–607) — and entropy rises significantly only for LLaMA (11.37→12.21⋆, 609–610; LLaMA's ARC-Easy true-class confidence falls furthest, 70.22→65.01⋆ with entropy 56.26→66.4⋆, 1713–1731). (5) Confidence change concentrates where the *uncompressed* model was already unsure: low-pre-confidence bins shift most, high-confidence samples least (abstract 69–72; Figure 1 discussion 724–732; Appendix C 1139–1142). (6) Full-vs-quantized Jensen-Shannon distance shrinks as scale grows, with LLaMA diverging from similarly sized ~7B peers (733–743). **Preserved vs degraded:** the accuracy deltas in Table 1 stay modest (mostly ≤ ~4 points; e.g. OPT-6.7B ARC-Easy −0.08, 384), whereas calibration error rises broadly (e.g. Mistral-7B-scale block ARC CE 7.94→+0.83, 137–138 — family attribution corrected 2026-08-25 after the number audit: the original "BLOOM-7.1B" label was unsupported; family markers are visual and lost to pdftotext, and BLOOM-7.1B's own ARC CE cell is 15.57 ↑1.06 at L267) and true-class confidence degrades significantly — i.e., what survives quantization best is argmax accuracy on samples the model was already confident about; what degrades is calibrated confidence, chiefly on uncertain inputs.
- **Cited in the report at:** Chapter 2 > 2.2
- **Limitations & caveats:** The authors scope the work themselves (767–782): only 4-bit post-training GPTQ (no quantization-aware training, no 2-/3-bit weights, no activation quantization, 768–773); zero-shot only, leaving few-shot effects untested (774–777); label-level distributions rather than token-level generation confidence (778–782). Additional caveats from the read: evaluation covers commonsense QA/classification-style scoring, not free generation or safety benchmarks; calibration metrics differ per task type (ECE binary vs ACE multi-class, 528–537), so CE values are not directly comparable across columns of Table 1; statistical testing is paired t-test at 0.05 without multiple-comparison correction (617–618); and the arXiv identifier attached to the repo bibliography entry could not be confirmed from this artifact. (My inference, flagged as such:) findings are GPTQ-specific and may not transfer verbatim to other 4-bit pipelines such as NF4.
- **Relevance to this FYP:** This paper supplies the confidence/calibration lens the FYP's interpretation framework needs: it shows quantization can leave aggregate accuracy nearly intact while significantly degrading calibrated confidence (442–448, 708–716), which cautions against reading small capability deltas as "no harm done" and motivates the FYP's practice of pairing ASR/OR changes with capability anchors. Its core structural finding — quantization loss varies by model family and scale, with LLaMA-7B an outlier among ~7B models (703–707, 740–743) — independently supports the FYP's design of evaluating matched fp16/NF4 pairs across four families instead of assuming uniform effects. Its "low-confidence samples are hit hardest" result (724–732) suggests per-item analyses of HarmBench/XSTest responses conditioned on baseline confidence would be more informative than aggregates alone (my inference, not something the paper tests). Two boundaries to keep honest when citing: the paper quantizes with GPTQ offline, whereas the FYP loads NF4/INT8 via bitsandbytes on the fly, so its magnitudes are analogous rather than transferable; and its benchmarks are commonsense QA, not safety suites — it evidences the *mechanism* (confidence/calibration shifts under quantization), not any safety-specific conclusion.
