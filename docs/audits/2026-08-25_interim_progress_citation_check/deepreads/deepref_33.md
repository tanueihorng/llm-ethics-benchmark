## Upgraded summary

- **Bibliography entry:** Audit list reads "[33] H. Dong et al., 'Durable quantization conditioned misalignment attack on large language models,' in Proc. ICLR, 2025." Title, venue, and year verify against the PDF (banner at extract 1); the leading author does **not**: the title block orders the paper Haowei Li → Peiran Dong → Song Guo with Li and Dong equal contributors (3–16, 50), so the correct short form is "H. Li et al." (and the full form "H. Li, P. Dong, and S. Guo"). The repo PDF filename ("[39] Dong et al. …") carries the same inverted attribution.
- **Repo PDF:** `Sources/[39] Dong et al. - Durable quantization conditioned misalignment attack on large language models.pdf`; extracted text `docs/audits/2026-08-25_interim_progress_citation_check/tmp/extract/ref_33.txt` (1,096 lines; 13 pages, footers 1–13).
- **Publication status:** Peer-reviewed archival venue — ICLR 2025 main conference, per the PDF's own banner "Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2025" (extract 1). No arXiv stamp appears anywhere in the extracted text.
- **Problem & context:** Users download open-source LLMs and quantize them locally for edge deployment; the paper posits an attacker who fine-tunes a pre-trained checkpoint locally to implant latent misalignment and re-uploads it, so the harm surfaces only after some victim quantizes (§1, 91–95; §3, 175–192). The attack must be **stealth** (full-precision behaviour and safety scores indistinguishable from the original, evading platform detectors) and **durable** (surviving victims' downstream fine-tuning and ICL safety prompting, neither of which the attacker controls) (193–201).
- **Approach:** Two-phase, two-site decomposition. *Training-time (attacker side):* Phase 1 fine-tunes the benign checkpoint into an explicitly harmful model on 100 "pure bad" red-teaming examples, 10 epochs, lr 4e-6 (502–504); Phase 2 performs constrained unlearning on 100 harmful instructions with rejective responses plus 500 benign pairs (≤5 epochs, lr 2e-5), combining four losses — L1 sigmoid-ratio unlearning, L2 learn-to-reject, L3 benign-task retention, and L4 = ‖θ̃imp − θ̃exp‖₂, a projected-gradient-descent term that keeps the *quantized* weights of the cleaned model close to those of the harmful one (243–317, 504–509). *Quantization-time (victim side):* nothing runs at the victim; L4 pre-shapes the full-precision weights so that the chosen quantizer's rounding (default int8; also evaluated NF4 and FP4) maps them into the misaligned basin — Figure 2's toy version tunes weights into the 5.5–6.0 band that is safe at FP but rounds unsafe (351–365, 593–596). *Durability component:* Contrastive Task Vectors computed from benign-vs-harmful fine-tunes of the same base, ratio τp−/τp+, clustered so the attack edits only safety-aligned parameters θp− and freezes task parameters θp+, which downstream SFT rarely touches (436–486). The paper itself flags that the attack targets one quantization precision at a time (913–916).
- **Key findings:** (1) *Stealth holds.* After Phase 2 the implicit harmful model's FP jailbreak ASR is ≤0.07 everywhere — e.g. InternLM2 0.03/0.01/0.01 and Gemma 0.06/0.00/0.01 across INT8/NF4/FP4 — comparable to the pre-trained model (Table 1, 533–571), and the model "evades detection by open-source platforms and detectors due to its alignment at full precision" (609–611). (2) *Quantization activates it.* Quantized ASR jumps to 0.94/0.94/0.93 (InternLM2), 0.95/0.90/0.90 (Gemma), 0.95/0.97/0.95 (Llama-2-7b-chat) (Table 1, 533–591); the paper summarizes this as success rates "exceeding 90%" although Gemma's NF4/FP4 cells equal 0.90 exactly (611–613). Pre-trained checkpoints quantized without the attack stay safe (Mp rows unchanged at 0.00–0.07, 535–543, 555–563, 575–583). (3) *All four losses are needed.* With L1+L2+L3+L4, InternLM2 ASR goes 0.03 (FP) → 0.94 (INT8) at an ACC cost of 0.4484 → 0.4121; dropping L4 (L1+L2+L3) or L3+L4 (L1+L2) leaves ASR 0.00 both precisions, and L1 alone produces degenerate output ("?????????????????") with no attack success (Table 2, 622–650). (4) *Utility cost shrinks with scale.* TruthfulQA MC2 of the implicit model matches the pre-trained model at FP, and the post-quantization drop is ~8% at 1.8B versus ~4% at 7B (Table 3, 662–725; prose 653–661, 846–847); PAIR against the attacked model at full precision gets ASR 0 (848–851). (5) *Durability under victim SFT (the headline for this audit).* Table 4's aggregate panel: after SFT on Alpaca, quantized ASR falls to 0.29 (InternLM2) / 0.13 (Gemma) for the ELQ baseline of Egashira et al. but retains 0.65 / 0.53 with CTV; after Dolly, 0.16 / 0.15 vs 0.41 / 0.56; before-SFT quantized ASR is 0.92–0.95 for both methods (740–785). The per-epoch Alpaca panel for InternLM2 (captionless float, 786–844) gives, over epochs 0–5, quantized ASR ELQ 0.94 → 0.44 → 0.14 → 0.08 → 0.06 → 0.08 versus Q-Misalign 0.92 → 0.78 → 0.24 → 0.23 → 0.17 → 0.11 — i.e. **after one Alpaca epoch, 0.78 (Q-Misalign) vs 0.44 (ELQ), independently re-verified here at extract 805–809 and 831–832** — with both decaying by epoch 5 yet Q-Misalign never dropping below 0.11 while FP ASR stays ≤0.17 throughout both panels. Prose framing: ELQ suffers catastrophic forgetting (>90% pre-SFT → <30% post-SFT quantized) while CTV buys roughly +30 pp on InternLM2 and +40 pp on Gemma, at <8 pp extra FP ASR, and "delays catastrophic amnesia" rather than preventing decay (852–861). (6) *Defenses discussed — none succeeds.* URIAL-style system-prompt ICL leaves the quantized implicit model 0.95–0.97 jailbreakable across all three models and precisions (Table 5, 871–896; prose 897–905); RLHF, adversarial training, and safety prompts are argued (related work, not experiments) to degrade on quantized models (922–933); the conclusion only calls for future "quantization-aware safety strategies" (950–951). Safe unlearning (Zhang et al., 2024) appears solely as a borrowed *component* of the attack's Phase 2 (244–246, 505), not as a defense evaluation.
- **Cited in the report at:** Chapter 2 > 2.2
- **Limitations & caveats:** Paper's own (§6, 907–916): coverage restricted to int8/NF4/FP4 edge-style schemes, leaving dynamic quantization untested; only harmful-content jailbreaking is measured, with bias/misinformation effects unexamined; the attack compromises **one target precision at a time**, not several simultaneously. Caveats added from this read (my observations, flagged as such): AdvBench ASRs are single point estimates — no confidence intervals or significance testing appear anywhere in the extract; Table 4's durability result covers only the two smaller models (no Llama-2-7b-chat rows exist in the table region of the extract, 740–785) and the per-epoch panel survives caption-less, so epoch indexing rests on axis ticks alone (786–844); the aggregate "SFT on Alpaca" cells (e.g. 0.65) match no single epoch value, implying a different unlabeled SFT budget than the epoch panel — the two cannot be reconciled from the text; Table 2's ACC ablation is InternLM2-only; and the epoch-panel decay means "durable" is relative — absolute quantized ASR still falls from 0.92 to 0.11 over five epochs.
- **Relevance to this FYP:** This is the adversarial-prepared extreme of the quantization-safety spectrum the FYP samples from the middle of: the FYP loads **unmodified** release checkpoints with on-the-fly BitsAndBytes NF4/INT8, while Q-Misalign requires an attacker to fine-tune and re-upload a deliberately crafted checkpoint (§3, 186–192). The paper therefore cannot predict anything about ordinary load-time quantization of unaltered models — indeed its own control shows plain quantized pre-trained models keep ASR at 0.00–0.07 (Table 1, 535–543 etc.) — which is useful defensive context for the FYP's null-ish ΔASR findings and for wording that avoids implying "quantization causes misalignment" in general (a D56-style fitness boundary worth keeping explicit when this reference is cited). Overlaps worth exploiting: same bitsandbytes quantizer family (LLM.int8, NF4 — 990–994, 593–596), similarly sized small models (1.8B–7B vs the FYP's 1.7–7.2B), and the shared lesson that capability metrics can look fine while safety behaviour shifts — though its utility probe is TruthfulQA MC2, not the FYP's MMLU/ARC anchors. Its finding that ICL safety prompts fail post-quantization (Table 5) cautions against proposing prompt-based mitigations in the FYP's discussion without quantization-specific evidence (my inference from their result, not something the FYP tests).
