## [1] AWQ: Activation-aware Weight Quantization
- **Bibliography entry:** [1] J. Lin, J. Tang, H. Tang, S. Yang, W.-M. Chen, W.-C. Wang, G. Xiao, X. Dang, C. Gan, and S. Han, “AWQ: Activation-aware weight quantization for on-device LLM compression and acceleration,” in Proc. Machine Learning and Systems (MLSys), 2024.
- **Repo PDF:** `Sources/[05] Lin et al. - AWQ - Activation-aware weight quantization for on-device LLM compression and acceleration.pdf` (no alternate version file exists in the repo)
- **Publication status:** Published at the 5th MLSys Conference (MLSys 2024), Santa Clara, CA, USA; the running footer reads "Proceedings of the 5th MLSys Conference" (p. 1 of the PDF).
- **Coverage:** mechanical basis pdftotext of the whole PDF (14 pages, ~10,448 words extracted). Summary depth: abstract, core method, and main results per batch protocol; appendices and secondary tables were not individually summarized (see CITATION_CHECK.md self-test record).
- **Problem & context:** On-device LLM serving is limited by edge-device memory and bandwidth, so the authors want a hardware-friendly, weight-only, low-bit quantization method that preserves accuracy without costly retraining or calibration-heavy optimization, and an inference stack to deploy it on consumer and mobile hardware.
- **Approach:** Activation-aware Weight Quantization (AWQ) starts from the observation that weights are not equally important: protecting only about 1% of salient weights can greatly reduce quantization error (Abstract). Instead of picking salient weights by weight magnitude, AWQ searches for a per-channel scaling chosen by observing activations, which is more robust. The method involves no backpropagation or reconstruction, so the authors argue it avoids overfitting to the calibration set and preserves generalization across domains and modalities, including instruction-tuned and multimodal LMs (Abstract). They evaluate language modeling plus coding and math tasks across Llama-2, MPT, Falcon and Mistral families, and build TinyChat, a 4-bit inference framework with kernel fusion for desktop GPUs, NVIDIA Jetson Orin, and Raspberry Pi.
- **Key findings:** TinyChat achieves more than 3x speedup over the HuggingFace FP16 implementation on both desktop and mobile GPUs and enables deploying 70B Llama-2 on mobile GPUs (Abstract); the overview figure states a 3-4x boost compared to FP16 (Figure 1). Against competing systems, TinyChat gives 1.2-3.0x speedup over existing systems on Jetson Orin and at least 2.6x over AutoGPTQ, and runs 7B models at 0.7 tokens/s on a Raspberry Pi 4B (Figure 10).
- **Cited in the report at:** Chapter 1: Introduction > 1.1 Background and Motivation, Chapter 2: Literature Review > 2.2 Quantization Methods and Capability Effects

## [2] GPTQ: Accurate Post-Training Quantization for Generative Pre-trained Transformers
- **Bibliography entry:** [2] E. Frantar, S. Ashkboos, T. Hoefler, and D. Alistarh, “GPTQ: Accurate post-training quantization for generative pre-trained transformers,” in Proc. International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR), 2023. arXiv:2210.17323.
- **Repo PDF:** `Sources/[06] Frantar et al. - GPTQ - Accurate post-training quantization for generative pre-trained transformers.pdf` (no alternate version file exists in the repo)
- **Publication status:** Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2023 (header on p. 1 of the PDF).
- **Coverage:** mechanical basis pdftotext of the whole PDF (16 pages, ~8,956 words extracted). Summary depth: abstract, core method, and main results per batch protocol; appendices and secondary tables were not individually summarized (see CITATION_CHECK.md self-test record).
- **Problem & context:** Generative transformer models such as GPT-3/OPT reach sizes whose FP16 inference does not fit on a single GPU, and existing post-training quantization had not been shown to work reliably at the hundred-billion-parameter scale, so the paper targets one-shot compression of very large generative models.
- **Approach:** GPTQ is a one-shot weight quantization method based on approximate second-order information. It works layer by layer and extends Optimal Brain Quantization (OBQ) with three changes: an "arbitrary order" insight that removes OBQ's cubic runtime dependency, lazy batch updates that postpone error compensation, and a Cholesky reformulation that keeps the inverse-Hessian computation numerically stable at scale (Section 3). The authors apply it to quantize OPT and BLOOM models up to 175B/176B parameters to 3 or 4 bits, and study the extreme 2-bit and ternary regimes.
- **Key findings:** GPTQ quantizes a 175B-parameter model in approximately four GPU hours with negligible accuracy degradation, more than doubling compression relative to prior one-shot methods and enabling, for the first time, a 175B-parameter model to run inside a single GPU for generative inference; end-to-end inference speedups over FP16 are around 3.25x on an A100 and 4.5x on an A6000 (Abstract). On OPT-175B WikiText2 perplexity, 3-bit round-to-nearest collapses to 7.3e3 while GPTQ reaches 8.68, and at 4 bits GPTQ reaches 8.37 versus 10.54 for round-to-nearest (Table 3).
- **Cited in the report at:** Chapter 1: Introduction > 1.1 Background and Motivation, Chapter 2: Literature Review > 2.2 Quantization Methods and Capability Effects

## [3] The case for 4-bit precision: k-bit Inference Scaling Laws
- **Bibliography entry:** [3] T. Dettmers and L. Zettlemoyer, “The case for 4-bit precision: k-bit inference scaling laws,” in Proc. International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), PMLR vol. 202, pp. 7750–7774, 2023. arXiv:2212.09720.
- **Repo PDF:** `Sources/[07] Dettmers et al. - The case for 4-bit precision - k-bit inference scaling laws.pdf` (no alternate version file exists in the repo)
- **Publication status:** Proceedings of the 40th International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML 2023), Honolulu, Hawaii, PMLR 202 (footer, p. 1 of the PDF).
- **Coverage:** mechanical basis pdftotext of the whole PDF (25 pages, ~11,414 words extracted). Summary depth: abstract, core method, and main results per batch protocol; appendices and secondary tables were not individually summarized (see CITATION_CHECK.md self-test record).
- **Problem & context:** A compressed model's quality depends jointly on parameter count and bit precision, so for a fixed budget of total model bits it is unclear whether to serve a smaller model at higher precision or a larger model at lower precision. The paper asks which bit width maximizes zero-shot accuracy for a given number of total bits.
- **Approach:** The authors fit bit-level inference scaling laws for zero-shot performance using more than 35,000 experiments with 16-bit inputs and k-bit parameters, spanning 19 million to 176 billion parameters across the BLOOM, OPT, and NeoX/Pythia families at 3 to 8-bit precision (Abstract). They vary the underlying quantization ingredients, including data types (integer versus float), block sizes, outlier-handling tricks such as proxy quantization, and quantization method settings, and correlate perplexity with zero-shot accuracy.
- **Key findings:** Reducing precision steadily improves the bit-level trade-off down to 4 bits, making 4-bit precision almost universally optimal for total model bits and zero-shot accuracy, while the trend reverses at 3 bits (Abstract, Figure 1); the one exception is BLOOM-176B, where 3-bit is slightly but not significantly better (results discussion). Practical recommendations are to always use 4-bit models with a small block size and to choose the right data type (Introduction; recommendations list). Small blocks help most at 3-5 bits: shrinking block size from 1024 to 64 costs only 0.24 extra bits per parameter (block-size discussion). The Pearson correlation between Pile CommonCrawl perplexity and zero-shot performance is -0.94 (correlation analysis).
- **Cited in the report at:** Chapter 1: Introduction > 1.1 Background and Motivation, Chapter 2: Literature Review > 2.2 Quantization Methods and Capability Effects

## [4] Qwen3 Technical Report
- **Bibliography entry:** [4] A. Yang et al. (Qwen Team), “Qwen3 technical report,” Alibaba Group, 2025. arXiv:2505.09388.
- **Repo PDF:** `Sources/[01] Yang et al. - Qwen3 technical report.pdf` (no alternate version file exists in the repo)
- **Publication status:** arXiv preprint (no archival venue shown); title page carries arXiv:2505.09388v1 dated 2025-05-15, authored by the Qwen Team, with all models released under Apache 2.0.
- **Coverage:** mechanical basis pdftotext of the whole PDF (35 pages, ~17,205 words extracted). Summary depth: abstract, core method, and main results per batch protocol; appendices and secondary tables were not individually summarized (see CITATION_CHECK.md self-test record).
- **Problem & context:** The report introduces the Qwen3 family, whose stated aim is to combine strong reasoning and efficient deployment in openly available models by unifying a deliberate "thinking mode" for complex multi-step reasoning and a "non-thinking mode" for rapid responses within a single model, instead of maintaining separate chat and reasoning models.
- **Approach:** Qwen3 spans dense models (0.6B, 1.7B, 4B, 8B, 14B, 32B) and Mixture-of-Experts models (30B-A3B and the 235B-A22B flagship), pre-trained on roughly 36 trillion tokens covering 119 languages and dialects (Abstract, Sections 1-3). Post-training proceeds in four stages: long chain-of-thought cold start, reasoning-focused reinforcement learning, thinking mode fusion, and general RL. Lightweight models are built via a Strong-to-Weak Distillation pipeline covering the five smaller dense models and 30B-A3B (post-training section). A thinking budget mechanism lets users cap reasoning tokens adaptively at inference time (Abstract, Figure 2).
- **Key findings:** The flagship Qwen3-235B-A22B in thinking mode outperforms DeepSeek-R1 on 17 of 23 evaluated benchmarks, and in non-thinking mode surpasses GPT-4o-2024-11-20 on 18 of 23 benchmarks (evaluation summary, Tables 11-12). Qwen3-32B outperforms QwQ-32B on 17 of 23 benchmarks (Table 13 summary). On Qwen3-8B, on-policy distillation beats reinforcement learning while using about one tenth of the GPU hours (Table 21). Smaller models such as 1.7B and 4B outperform comparably sized baselines in both modes (Tables 19-20).
- **Cited in the report at:** Chapter 1: Introduction > 1.1 Background and Motivation

## [5] The Llama 3 herd of models
- **Bibliography entry:** [5] A. Grattafiori et al. (Meta AI), “The Llama 3 herd of models,” Meta AI, 2024. arXiv:2407.21783.
- **Repo PDF:** `Sources/[02] Grattafiori et al. - The Llama 3 herd of models.pdf` (extracted text is arXiv:2407.21783v3, dated 23 Nov 2024; no alternate version file in the repo)
- **Publication status:** arXiv preprint (no archival venue shown)
- **Coverage:** mechanical basis pdftotext of the whole PDF (92 pages, ~53,645 words extracted). Summary depth: abstract, core method, and main results per batch protocol; appendices and secondary tables were not individually summarized (see CITATION_CHECK.md self-test record).
- **Problem & context:** Modern AI systems rest on foundation models, but open models trail closed frontier systems in capability and safety tooling. Meta presents Llama 3, a herd of language models intended to narrow that gap while natively supporting multilinguality, coding, reasoning, and tool usage.
- **Approach:** The flagship is a standard dense Transformer with 405B parameters, pre-trained on about 15T multilingual tokens (15.6T text tokens) at roughly 3.8×10^25 FLOPs, then run through a continued pre-training stage that extends the context window to 128K tokens. Smaller 8B and 70B variants are trained well beyond compute-optimal budgets so they perform better at equal inference cost. Post-training proceeds in multiple rounds of supervised finetuning followed by Direct Preference Optimization, supported by a reward model, rejection sampling, and curated human preference data. Safety mitigations are folded into post-training, and the released package includes Llama Guard 3 for input and output safety. Image, video, and speech extensions via compositional encoders are reported but not released.
- **Key findings:** The abstract reports quality comparable to GPT-4 across many tasks, with Table 2 comparing the finetuned 8B, 70B, and 405B models against competitors on benchmarks including MMLU, GSM8K, ARC Challenge, and GPQA. In safety evaluations (§5.4) the team measures violation rate against false refusal rate on internal benchmarks totalling over 4000 prompts, reporting a competitive balance against anonymised API-based and open competitors (§5.4.4, Figure 21), while footnote 13 concedes those internal numbers are not externally reproducible.
- **Cited in the report at:** Chapter 1: Introduction > 1.1 Background and Motivation

## [6] Phi-4-Mini technical report
- **Bibliography entry:** [6] A. Abouelenin et al., “Phi-4-Mini technical report: Compact yet powerful multimodal language models via Mixture-of-LoRAs,” Microsoft, 2025. arXiv:2503.01743.
- **Repo PDF:** `Sources/[03] Abouelenin et al. - Phi-4-Mini technical report - Compact yet powerful multimodal language models via Mixture-of-LoRAs.pdf` (extracted text is arXiv:2503.01743v2, dated 7 Mar 2025; no alternate version file in the repo)
- **Publication status:** arXiv preprint (no archival venue shown)
- **Coverage:** mechanical basis pdftotext of the whole PDF (39 pages, ~16,308 words extracted). Summary depth: abstract, core method, and main results per batch protocol; appendices and secondary tables were not individually summarized (see CITATION_CHECK.md self-test record).
- **Problem & context:** Small language models must run cheaply yet usually trail larger ones on reasoning-heavy work. Microsoft extends the Phi family recipe of carefully curated synthetic data to a compact model and adds vision and speech without degrading its language ability.
- **Approach:** Phi-4-Mini is a decoder-only Transformer with 32 layers, hidden size 3072, a 200,064-entry vocabulary, grouped query attention (24 query heads, 8 key/value heads), and 128K context via LongRoPE (§2.1). Pre-training draws on a filtered five-trillion-token corpus enriched with synthetic math, code, and reasoning data (§3.1.1). Phi-4-Multimodal freezes this backbone and trains modality-specific LoRA adapters alongside vision and audio encoders under a Mixture-of-LoRAs design (§1, §2.2). A separate experimental reasoning variant is continued-pre-trained on about 60 billion chain-of-thought tokens, fine-tuned on roughly 200K curated CoT samples, then tuned by DPO on 300K preference pairs.
- **Key findings:** The abstract claims it significantly outperforms recent similar-size open models and matches models twice its size on math and coding, with Tables 7 and 8 benchmarking against the Llama-3.2, Qwen2.5, Ministral, Gemma, and Phi-3.5-Mini lines. Its speech LoRA, only 460M parameters, reportedly ranked first on the OpenASR leaderboard at publication time (Abstract). A responsible-AI section reports XSTest-derived refusal metrics separating inappropriate-prompt refusal (IPRR) from valid-prompt refusal (VPRR) in Table 12, and harmful-content defect rates averaged over four categories that improve on Phi-3.5-mini (Table 13).
- **Cited in the report at:** Chapter 1: Introduction > 1.1 Background and Motivation

## [7] Mistral 7B
- **Bibliography entry:** [7] A. Q. Jiang et al., “Mistral 7B,” Mistral AI, 2023. arXiv:2310.06825.
- **Repo PDF:** `Sources/[51] Jiang et al. - Mistral 7B.pdf` (no alternate version file exists in the repo)
- **Publication status:** arXiv preprint (no archival venue shown); title page carries arXiv:2310.06825v1 dated 10 Oct 2023; the models are released under the Apache 2.0 license.
- **Coverage:** mechanical basis pdftotext of the whole PDF (9 pages, ~3,837 words extracted). Summary depth: abstract, core method, and main results per batch protocol; appendices and secondary tables were not individually summarized (see CITATION_CHECK.md self-test record).
- **Problem & context:** The paper argues that careful architecture design can deliver performance comparable to much larger models at a fraction of the inference cost, addressing the rising computational cost and latency of scaled-up LLMs in practical deployments.
- **Approach:** Mistral 7B is a 7-billion-parameter model built around two architectural choices: grouped-query attention (GQA), which speeds inference and lowers decoder memory so larger batches become feasible, and sliding window attention (SWA), which controls attention cost for sequences of arbitrary length (Abstract, Section 2). The authors release reference implementations for cloud and local deployment and demonstrate fine-tuning on public instruction datasets without proprietary data to produce Mistral 7B Instruct (Sections 1, 3-4). They benchmark against Llama 1/2 and Code-Llama on knowledge, commonsense reasoning, math, and code, and add a guardrailing study.
- **Key findings:** Mistral 7B outperforms the best open 13B model (Llama 2) across all evaluated benchmarks and the best released 34B model (Llama 1) in reasoning, mathematics, and code generation (Abstract); its pretrained MMLU score is 60.1%, with the table caption stating it beats Llama 2 13B on all reported metrics (Table 2). The instruct variant scores 6.84 +/- 0.07 on MT-Bench, above all other 7B chat models and comparable to 13B chat models (Table 3). With a system prompt the base model declines 100% of 175 unsafe prompts, and self-reflection content moderation reaches 99.4% precision at 95.6% recall (guardrails section).
- **Cited in the report at:** Chapter 1: Introduction > 1.1 Background and Motivation, Chapter 3: Methodology and Experimental Setup > 3.2 Models and Precision Conditions

## [8] A comprehensive evaluation of quantization strategies for large language models
- **Bibliography entry:** [8] R. Jin, J. Du, W. Huang, et al., "A comprehensive evaluation of quantization strategies for large language models," in Findings of ACL, 2024 — matches the paper: title L1–L2; authors Renren Jin, Jiangcun Du, Wuwei Huang, Wei Liu, Jian Luan, Bin Wang, Deyi Xiong (L3–L4); venue footer "Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: ACL 2024, pages 12186–12215" (L101–L103). Numbering alert: in the current report builder this same entry is bracket **[10]** (builder L1078), not [8]; see Discrepancy check.
- **Repo PDF:** `Sources/[08] Jin et al. - A comprehensive evaluation of quantization strategies for large language models.pdf` (present in `Sources/`, verified this session via directory listing); full-text extract at `docs/audits/2026-08-25_interim_progress_citation_check/tmp/extract/ref_8.txt` (6,040 lines).
- **Publication status:** Peer-reviewed archival venue, printed in the PDF itself: Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: ACL 2024, pages 12186–12215, August 11–16, 2024, ©2024 Association for Computational Linguistics (L101–L103). Work performed during the first author's internship at Xiaomi AI Lab (L49); code released at github.com/cordercorder/quant_eval (L325–L326).
- **Coverage:** Full every-sentence read: 24 sections READ-FULL, 0 SKIMMED, 0 UNREADABLE out of ~24 mapped (mechanical basis: pdftotext of the whole PDF (30 pages, ~14,050 words extracted)). Audit trail incl. per-section table and discrepancy checks: `docs/audits/2026-08-25_interim_progress_citation_check/deepreads/fullref_8.md`.
- **Problem & context:** Three evaluation gaps motivate the paper: most PTQ studies evaluate pre-trained rather than instruction-tuned LLMs (L20–L21, L289–L293); quantized-model evaluation is usually limited to language modeling plus a few classification tasks (L23–L24, L294–L298); and the correlation between quantized LLMs' perplexity and their downstream benchmark performance is poorly understood (L21–L23, L298–L304).
- **Approach:** A structured framework over three dimensions — knowledge & capacity, alignment, efficiency (L25–L27, L398–L403) — applied to ten benchmarks (Table 1, L105–L267): MMLU (14,042 test samples, L127), C-EVAL (12,342), FLORES-200 En↔Zh translation BLEU (1,012, L151), CNN/DailyMail and XSum summarisation ROUGE (11,490/L169, 11,334/L179), GSM8K (1,319, L189), SNLI (10,000, L199), and — for alignment, mapped onto Askell et al.'s helpful/honest/harmless criteria (L904–L909) — FollowBench instruction-following HSR/SSR/CSL (820, L209; GPT-4-as-judge for SSR, L253), TruthfulQA MC1 (817, L220), BBQ social bias (58,492, L230). Models are the instruction-tuned Qwen-Chat family at 7B/14B/72B (L926–L936); methods are post-training quantization only: LLM.int8() (8-bit only), GPTQ and SpQR ("8, 4, 3, and 2" bits, L1262–L1264), calibrated on 128 random examples from Alpaca-style instruction data (Taori et al. 2023; Peng et al. 2023, L1265–L1267); SpQR runs via fake quantization since no CUDA kernel existed (L2913–L2915). Efficiency is measured as memory (GB) and decoding speed (tokens/s) at 256-token input/512-token output on A100 80GB SXM GPUs (L900–L902, L2138). Scope nuance: all three evaluated methods quantize weights while activations stay high precision (L2287–L2291); weight-activation schemes such as SmoothQuant appear only in Related Work (L349–L352), so the study compares weight-side PTQ strategies across bit widths, not weight-only vs weight-activation pipelines.
- **Key findings:** (1) Capability: 8-bit is near-lossless; 4-bit keeps performance comparable to non-quantized counterparts on most benchmarks; degradation starts at 3 bits or lower, and 2-bit GPTQ collapses into incoherent, instruction-blind text (L1700–L1716; e.g., Qwen-7B MMLU average 55.80 → 23.74⋆ GPTQ vs 52.49⋆ SpQR at 2-bit, L4236/L4321–L4322). (2) Perplexity is a reliable proxy: average absolute Pearson correlation with benchmark performance 0.7895 over 4-/3-bit GPTQ+SpQR models (Table 3, L1859–L2032; L2023–L2027); 2-bit GPTQ perplexity exceeds 38,000 (L1726–L1727), e.g. WikiText2 123030.34 for Qwen-7B (Table 7, L3859). (3) SpQR's 2-bit survival depends on isolating unstructured outlier weights kept at 16-bit: dropping isolation inflates perplexity even at group size 16, whereas enlarging group size "from 16 to 128" costs little (controlled experiments, L2046–L2060, Table 4 L2139–L2210); high-precision outliers grow from 0.003% (8-bit) to 11.336% (2-bit) for Qwen-7B (Table 5, L2234–L2237). (4) Efficiency: GPTQ/LLM.int8() cut memory (Qwen-72B: 138.44 GB → 77.85 GB at 8-bit GPTQ, L758/L793) but generally do not speed up decoding because activations remain high precision and hardware lacks mixed-precision acceleration — only the 72B model shows significant speed-up, at 4-bit (L2279–L2295); SpQR's studied implementation saves no memory at all (fake quantization, L2265–L2271); baseline Qwen-7B runs 15.14 GB / 37.67 tokens-per-s (L468/L470). (5) At similar memory, prefer a larger quantized model: Qwen-14B 8-bit GPTQ (17.92 GB; corrected 2026-08-25 — the number audit caught the original text attaching LLM.int8()'s 15.91 GB cell to GPTQ; INT-8 row reads LLM.int8 15.91 / GPTQ 17.92, Table 8) or 4-bit GPTQ (12.03 GB) costs about the same as Qwen-7B BFloat16 (15.14 GB) yet outperforms it on most benchmarks (L2300–L2307; Table 8 L4086–L4117). On ability vs safety: capability is preserved down to 4 bits for all three methods, with SpQR clearly best at extreme (≤3-bit, especially 2-bit) compression; alignment signals are mixed — social bias shows no consistent direction under quantization (can exacerbate or alleviate, L2365–L2367), truthfulness can fall significantly (Qwen-7B TruthfulQA MC1 0.38 → 0.30⋆ at 2-bit GPTQ, Table 13 L5404–L5467; also flagged in Ethical Considerations L2368–L2372), and instruction-following degrades at low bits (Qwen-7B HSR 0.40 → 0.00 at 2-bit GPTQ, Table 14 L5636–L5704). The paper measures no harmfulness/refusal-compliance metric anywhere (no HarmBench-style test in the extract), so it makes no claim about attack success rate.
- **Cited in the report at:** Chapter 1: Introduction > 1.1 Background and Motivation, Chapter 2: Literature Review > 2.2 Quantization Methods and Capability Effects
- **Limitations & caveats:** The authors flag possible pre-training/test contamination that could inflate scores and limit generalization to other benchmarks (L2337–L2344); experiments cover only the Qwen-Chat family, so findings may not transfer to other LLMs given differences in data, hyperparameters, and architectures (L2345–L2354). Additional caveats material to reuse: SpQR was run fake-quantized, so its measured memory/speed reflects the implementation, not the method's potential (L2265–L2271, L2909–L2916); the perplexity–performance link is correlational, computed on averaged scores of 4-/3-bit models (Table 3 caption L2029–L2032); all benchmarks are rule-based except FollowBench's GPT-4-judged SSR component (Table 1, L248–L253); efficiency uses one GPU type (A100 80GB SXM) and one fixed token profile (256-in/512-out, L900–L902, L2138); and the evaluated methods do not include NF4/QLoRA quantization itself (NF4 appears only as related work, L354–L355), nor any harmfulness/safety-refusal benchmark.
- **Relevance to this FYP:** Indirect but supportive. The paper's central result — 4-bit weight-side PTQ broadly preserves benchmark ability while ≤3-bit is where behaviour breaks (L1700–L1716, Conclusion L2324–L2326) — is consistent with this FYP's operating range (NF4 4-bit main study plus an INT8 point) and with its finding that quantization costs show up on the capability side rather than as safety erosion; its perplexity-as-proxy result (avg |Pearson| 0.7895, L2026) cautions that capability anchors must be watched even when headline scores hold. It cannot corroborate the FYP's safety-null directly: the models are far larger (7B–72B chat models vs this project's ≈1.7–7.2B pairs), the methods differ (GPTQ/SpQR/LLM.int8(), not BitsAndBytes NF4), and no harmfulness or over-refusal metric is evaluated — its "alignment" axis (instruction following, truthfulness, social bias) is complementary evidence only. Its engineering conclusion that quantized inference often slows down without kernel/hardware support (L2279–L2299) matches this project's on-the-fly BitsAndBytes loading context, where memory savings, not speed, are the expected benefit. Citation-fitness note: the current v5 report's single use of this source (§2.4, "headline benchmark scores are largely retained at four bits", builder L446) is supported by the paper (Abstract L29–L31; L1700–L1716; L2324–L2326).

## [9] Evaluating quantized large language models
- **Bibliography entry:** [9] S. Li, X. Ning, L. Wang, T. Liu, X. Shi, S. Yan, G. Dai, H. Yang, and Y. Wang, "Evaluating quantized large language models," in Proc. International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), 2024. arXiv:2402.18158.
- **Repo PDF:** `Sources/[09] Li et al. - Evaluating quantized large language models.pdf`
- **Publication status:** Proceedings of the 41st International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML 2024), Vienna, PMLR 235 — venue footer visible in the extracted PDF text (L73–75).
- **Coverage:** Full every-sentence read: 24 sections READ-FULL, 0 SKIMMED, 0 UNREADABLE out of ~24 mapped (mechanical basis: pdftotext of the whole PDF (45 pages, ~25,462 words extracted)). Audit trail incl. per-section table and discrepancy checks: `docs/audits/2026-08-25_interim_progress_citation_check/deepreads/fullref_9.md`.
- **Problem & context:** Prior quantization evaluations concentrated on language modelling and zero-shot understanding/emergent tasks, leaving dialogue, long-context processing and trustworthiness essentially untested, and gave practitioners no cross-model guidance on which tensors to quantize at which bit-widths (L121–125, L138–151). This paper positions itself as the comprehensive four-dimension audit: task breadth, model-family breadth, tensor type (Weight vs Activation vs KV Cache), and SOTA-method applicability (AWQ, SmoothQuant) (L138–153). Code released at thu-nics/qllm-eval (L25–26).
- **Approach:** PTQ applied separately to Weights, Activations and KV Cache over 11 model families (OPT, LLaMA2, Falcon, Bloomz, Mistral, ChatGLM, Vicuna, LongChat, StableLM, Gemma, Mamba), 125M–180B parameters, across five task types: basic NLP, emergent ability, trustworthiness, dialogue, long-context (L16–24). Benchmark suite (Table 2): basic NLP on CHID/Winogrande/RACE/LAMBADA/SIQA/PIQA; emergent abilities on MMLU (14,079 test questions, L211), CEval, GSM8K, StrategyQA, ARC, HellaSwag; trustworthiness on ETHICS (15,160, L257), TruthfulQA (817, L263) and AdvGLUE (738, L269); dialogue on MT-bench (80 two-turn questions, L277) scored by GPT-4-0613 single-answer grading on a 1–10 scale (L1228–1231); long-context on LongEval key-value retrieval up to 16K tokens (3,000 samples, L287, L1602) and multi-document QA up to 6K tokens (700 samples, L293, L1604–1605). - **Limitations & caveats:** The paper's own §8: post-training quantization only (no QAT); no ablations of hyperparameters such as group size; incomplete coverage of newly released small models (1B–3B); bit-width recommendations are specific to the evaluated LLMs/tasks and might not transfer (L1624–1632). - **Relevance to this FYP:** Anchors the FYP's premise that 4-bit weight-only sits in this paper's near-lossless regime (within ~2%) with degradation concentrating below it (L479–487, L110–119), consistent with using NF4 4-bit as the main study condition plus an INT8 point.
