## [1] AWQ: Activation-aware Weight Quantization
- **Bibliography entry:** [1] J. Lin, J. Tang, H. Tang, S. Yang, W.-M. Chen, W.-C. Wang, G. Xiao, X. Dang, C. Gan, and S. Han, “AWQ: Activation-aware weight quantization for on-device LLM compression and acceleration,” in Proc. Machine Learning and Systems (MLSys), 2024.
- **Repo PDF:** `Sources/[05] Lin et al. - AWQ - Activation-aware weight quantization for on-device LLM compression and acceleration.pdf` (no alternate version file exists in the repo)
- **Publication status:** Published at the 5th MLSys Conference (MLSys 2024), Santa Clara, CA, USA; the running footer reads "Proceedings of the 5th MLSys Conference" (p. 1 of the PDF).
- **Problem & context:** On-device LLM serving is limited by edge-device memory and bandwidth, so the authors want a hardware-friendly, weight-only, low-bit quantization method that preserves accuracy without costly retraining or calibration-heavy optimization, and an inference stack to deploy it on consumer and mobile hardware.
- **Approach:** Activation-aware Weight Quantization (AWQ) starts from the observation that weights are not equally important: protecting only about 1% of salient weights can greatly reduce quantization error (Abstract). Instead of picking salient weights by weight magnitude, AWQ searches for a per-channel scaling chosen by observing activations, which is more robust. The method involves no backpropagation or reconstruction, so the authors argue it avoids overfitting to the calibration set and preserves generalization across domains and modalities, including instruction-tuned and multimodal LMs (Abstract). They evaluate language modeling plus coding and math tasks across Llama-2, MPT, Falcon and Mistral families, and build TinyChat, a 4-bit inference framework with kernel fusion for desktop GPUs, NVIDIA Jetson Orin, and Raspberry Pi.
- **Key findings:** TinyChat achieves more than 3x speedup over the HuggingFace FP16 implementation on both desktop and mobile GPUs and enables deploying 70B Llama-2 on mobile GPUs (Abstract); the overview figure states a 3-4x boost compared to FP16 (Figure 1). Against competing systems, TinyChat gives 1.2-3.0x speedup over existing systems on Jetson Orin and at least 2.6x over AutoGPTQ, and runs 7B models at 0.7 tokens/s on a Raspberry Pi 4B (Figure 10).
- **Cited in the report at:** Chapter 1: Introduction > 1.1 Background and Motivation, Chapter 2: Literature Review > 2.2 Quantization Methods and Capability Effects

## [2] GPTQ: Accurate Post-Training Quantization for Generative Pre-trained Transformers
- **Bibliography entry:** [2] E. Frantar, S. Ashkboos, T. Hoefler, and D. Alistarh, “GPTQ: Accurate post-training quantization for generative pre-trained transformers,” in Proc. International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR), 2023. arXiv:2210.17323.
- **Repo PDF:** `Sources/[06] Frantar et al. - GPTQ - Accurate post-training quantization for generative pre-trained transformers.pdf` (no alternate version file exists in the repo)
- **Publication status:** Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2023 (header on p. 1 of the PDF).
- **Problem & context:** Generative transformer models such as GPT-3/OPT reach sizes whose FP16 inference does not fit on a single GPU, and existing post-training quantization had not been shown to work reliably at the hundred-billion-parameter scale, so the paper targets one-shot compression of very large generative models.
- **Approach:** GPTQ is a one-shot weight quantization method based on approximate second-order information. It works layer by layer and extends Optimal Brain Quantization (OBQ) with three changes: an "arbitrary order" insight that removes OBQ's cubic runtime dependency, lazy batch updates that postpone error compensation, and a Cholesky reformulation that keeps the inverse-Hessian computation numerically stable at scale (Section 3). The authors apply it to quantize OPT and BLOOM models up to 175B/176B parameters to 3 or 4 bits, and study the extreme 2-bit and ternary regimes.
- **Key findings:** GPTQ quantizes a 175B-parameter model in approximately four GPU hours with negligible accuracy degradation, more than doubling compression relative to prior one-shot methods and enabling, for the first time, a 175B-parameter model to run inside a single GPU for generative inference; end-to-end inference speedups over FP16 are around 3.25x on an A100 and 4.5x on an A6000 (Abstract). On OPT-175B WikiText2 perplexity, 3-bit round-to-nearest collapses to 7.3e3 while GPTQ reaches 8.68, and at 4 bits GPTQ reaches 8.37 versus 10.54 for round-to-nearest (Table 3).
- **Cited in the report at:** Chapter 2: Literature Review > 2.2 Quantization Methods and Capability Effects

## [3] The case for 4-bit precision: k-bit Inference Scaling Laws
- **Bibliography entry:** [3] T. Dettmers and L. Zettlemoyer, “The case for 4-bit precision: k-bit inference scaling laws,” in Proc. International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), PMLR vol. 202, pp. 7750–7774, 2023. arXiv:2212.09720.
- **Repo PDF:** `Sources/[07] Dettmers et al. - The case for 4-bit precision - k-bit inference scaling laws.pdf` (no alternate version file exists in the repo)
- **Publication status:** Proceedings of the 40th International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML 2023), Honolulu, Hawaii, PMLR 202 (footer, p. 1 of the PDF).
- **Problem & context:** A compressed model's quality depends jointly on parameter count and bit precision, so for a fixed budget of total model bits it is unclear whether to serve a smaller model at higher precision or a larger model at lower precision. The paper asks which bit width maximizes zero-shot accuracy for a given number of total bits.
- **Approach:** The authors fit bit-level inference scaling laws for zero-shot performance using more than 35,000 experiments with 16-bit inputs and k-bit parameters, spanning 19 million to 176 billion parameters across the BLOOM, OPT, and NeoX/Pythia families at 3 to 8-bit precision (Abstract). They vary the underlying quantization ingredients, including data types (integer versus float), block sizes, outlier-handling tricks such as proxy quantization, and quantization method settings, and correlate perplexity with zero-shot accuracy.
- **Key findings:** Reducing precision steadily improves the bit-level trade-off down to 4 bits, making 4-bit precision almost universally optimal for total model bits and zero-shot accuracy, while the trend reverses at 3 bits (Abstract, Figure 1); the one exception is BLOOM-176B, where 3-bit is slightly but not significantly better (results discussion). Practical recommendations are to always use 4-bit models with a small block size and to choose the right data type (Introduction; recommendations list). Small blocks help most at 3-5 bits: shrinking block size from 1024 to 64 costs only 0.24 extra bits per parameter (block-size discussion). The Pearson correlation between Pile CommonCrawl perplexity and zero-shot performance is -0.94 (correlation analysis).
- **Cited in the report at:** Chapter 1: Introduction > 1.1 Background and Motivation, Chapter 2: Literature Review > 2.2 Quantization Methods and Capability Effects

## [4] Qwen3 Technical Report
- **Bibliography entry:** [4] A. Yang et al. (Qwen Team), “Qwen3 technical report,” Alibaba Group, 2025. arXiv:2505.09388.
- **Repo PDF:** `Sources/[01] Yang et al. - Qwen3 technical report.pdf` (no alternate version file exists in the repo)
- **Publication status:** arXiv preprint (no archival venue shown); title page carries arXiv:2505.09388v1 dated 2025-05-15, authored by the Qwen Team, with all models released under Apache 2.0.
- **Problem & context:** The report introduces the Qwen3 family, whose stated aim is to combine strong reasoning and efficient deployment in openly available models by unifying a deliberate "thinking mode" for complex multi-step reasoning and a "non-thinking mode" for rapid responses within a single model, instead of maintaining separate chat and reasoning models.
- **Approach:** Qwen3 spans dense models (0.6B, 1.7B, 4B, 8B, 14B, 32B) and Mixture-of-Experts models (30B-A3B and the 235B-A22B flagship), pre-trained on roughly 36 trillion tokens covering 119 languages and dialects (Abstract, Sections 1-3). Post-training proceeds in four stages: long chain-of-thought cold start, reasoning-focused reinforcement learning, thinking mode fusion, and general RL. Lightweight models are built via a Strong-to-Weak Distillation pipeline covering the five smaller dense models and 30B-A3B (post-training section). A thinking budget mechanism lets users cap reasoning tokens adaptively at inference time (Abstract, Figure 2).
- **Key findings:** The flagship Qwen3-235B-A22B in thinking mode outperforms DeepSeek-R1 on 17 of 23 evaluated benchmarks, and in non-thinking mode surpasses GPT-4o-2024-11-20 on 18 of 23 benchmarks (evaluation summary, Tables 11-12). Qwen3-32B outperforms QwQ-32B on 17 of 23 benchmarks (Table 13 summary). On Qwen3-8B, on-policy distillation beats reinforcement learning while using about one tenth of the GPU hours (Table 21). Smaller models such as 1.7B and 4B outperform comparably sized baselines in both modes (Tables 19-20).
- **Cited in the report at:** Chapter 1: Introduction > 1.1 Background and Motivation

## [7] Mistral 7B
- **Bibliography entry:** [7] A. Q. Jiang et al., “Mistral 7B,” Mistral AI, 2023. arXiv:2310.06825.
- **Repo PDF:** `Sources/[51] Jiang et al. - Mistral 7B.pdf` (no alternate version file exists in the repo)
- **Publication status:** arXiv preprint (no archival venue shown); title page carries arXiv:2310.06825v1 dated 10 Oct 2023; the models are released under the Apache 2.0 license.
- **Problem & context:** The paper argues that careful architecture design can deliver performance comparable to much larger models at a fraction of the inference cost, addressing the rising computational cost and latency of scaled-up LLMs in practical deployments.
- **Approach:** Mistral 7B is a 7-billion-parameter model built around two architectural choices: grouped-query attention (GQA), which speeds inference and lowers decoder memory so larger batches become feasible, and sliding window attention (SWA), which controls attention cost for sequences of arbitrary length (Abstract, Section 2). The authors release reference implementations for cloud and local deployment and demonstrate fine-tuning on public instruction datasets without proprietary data to produce Mistral 7B Instruct (Sections 1, 3-4). They benchmark against Llama 1/2 and Code-Llama on knowledge, commonsense reasoning, math, and code, and add a guardrailing study.
- **Key findings:** Mistral 7B outperforms the best open 13B model (Llama 2) across all evaluated benchmarks and the best released 34B model (Llama 1) in reasoning, mathematics, and code generation (Abstract); its pretrained MMLU score is 60.1%, with the table caption stating it beats Llama 2 13B on all reported metrics (Table 2). The instruct variant scores 6.84 +/- 0.07 on MT-Bench, above all other 7B chat models and comparable to 13B chat models (Table 3). With a system prompt the base model declines 100% of 175 unsafe prompts, and self-reflection content moderation reaches 99.4% precision at 95.6% recall (guardrails section).
- **Cited in the report at:** Chapter 1: Introduction > 1.1 Background and Motivation, Chapter 3: Methodology and Experimental Setup > 3.2 Models and Precision Conditions

## [22] SmoothQuant: Accurate and Efficient Post-Training Quantization for Large Language Models
- **Bibliography entry:** [22] G. Xiao, J. Lin, M. Seznec, H. Wu, J. Demouth, and S. Han, “SmoothQuant: Accurate and efficient post-training quantization for large language models,” in Proc. International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), PMLR vol. 202, 2023.
- **Repo PDF:** `Sources/[30] Xiao et al. - SmoothQuant - Accurate and efficient post-training quantization for large language models.pdf` (no alternate version file exists in the repo)
- **Publication status:** Proceedings of the 40th International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML 2023), Honolulu, Hawaii, PMLR 202 (footer, p. 1 of the PDF).
- **Problem & context:** Beyond roughly 6.7B parameters, systematic activation outliers make activations hard to quantize, so prior W8A8 schemes either lose accuracy (ZeroQuant fails on OPT-175B) or stay accurate but run slowly because they keep computations in higher precision (LLM.int8()). The paper seeks INT8 weights-and-activations quantization that is simultaneously accurate and hardware-efficient.
- **Approach:** SmoothQuant is a training-free post-training method that applies a mathematically equivalent per-channel transformation which "smooths" activation magnitudes by migrating quantization difficulty from activations into the weights, controlled by a migration strength alpha; alpha = 0.5 is the sweet spot for OPT and BLOOM models, with larger values for GLM and LLaMA (Abstract; alpha ablation). It covers all matrix multiplications and integrates into PyTorch and FasterTransformer with three presets (O1-O3). Models studied include OPT-175B, BLOOM-176B, GLM-130B, LLaMA, and MT-NLG 530B.
- **Key findings:** On OPT-175B, average zero-shot accuracy is 66.9% for FP16 versus 66.8% for the most aggressive SmoothQuant-O3, while ZeroQuant drops to 35.5%; WikiText perplexity rises only from 10.99 to 11.17 (Table 3). Serving achieves up to 1.56x speedup and halves memory, letting MT-NLG 530B run within a single 8-GPU node for the first time (Abstract, Tables 8-9, conclusion).
- **Cited in the report at:** Chapter 2: Literature Review > 2.2 Quantization Methods and Capability Effects

## [23] SparseGPT: Massive Language Models Can Be Accurately Pruned in One-Shot
- **Bibliography entry:** [23] E. Frantar and D. Alistarh, “SparseGPT: Massive language models can be accurately pruned in one-shot,” in Proc. International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), PMLR vol. 202, 2023.
- **Repo PDF:** `Sources/[31] Frantar et al. - SparseGPT - Massive language models can be accurately pruned in one-shot.pdf` (no alternate version file exists in the repo)
- **Publication status:** Proceedings of the 40th International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML 2023), Honolulu, Hawaii, PMLR 202 (footer, p. 1 of the PDF).
- **Problem & context:** Accurate pruning traditionally requires extensive retraining, which is prohibitive for GPT-scale models, and even prior one-shot solvers become impractical beyond a few billion parameters, so essentially no accurate pruning results existed for hundred-billion-parameter models.
- **Approach:** SparseGPT reduces layer-wise pruning to very large instances of sparse regression, solved by a new approximate second-order solver adapted from OBS-style updates and inheriting efficiency techniques from GPTQ, including Cholesky-based bookkeeping and lazy batch updates; mask selection is adaptive over blocks of columns (Sections 2-3). It runs on a single GPU, generalizes from unstructured sparsity to semi-structured 2:4 and 4:8 patterns, and can prune and quantize in a single pass (Abstract, joint-compression section). Experiments cover OPT (125M to 175B) and BLOOM (up to 176B).
- **Key findings:** SparseGPT executes on OPT-175B and BLOOM-176B in under 4.5 hours, reaching 50-60% unstructured sparsity with negligible perplexity increase, meaning more than 100 billion weights can be ignored at inference time (Abstract, conclusion). At the largest scales, semi-structured 4:8 and 2:4 variants raise perplexity by only 0.11 and 0.39 respectively (large-scale results). Joint 50% sparsity with 4-bit quantization yields perplexity 8.29 versus 8.68 for a size-equivalent 3-bit GPTQ model at 175B (joint sparsification and quantization result). Accuracy degrades less for larger models.
- **Cited in the report at:** Chapter 2: Literature Review > 2.2 Quantization Methods and Capability Effects

## [24] QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs
- **Bibliography entry:** [24] T. Dettmers, A. Pagnoni, A. Holtzman, and L. Zettlemoyer, “QLoRA: Efficient finetuning of quantized LLMs,” in Proc. NeurIPS, 2023. arXiv:2305.14314. (Introduces NF4 and the bitsandbytes integration.)
- **Repo PDF:** `Sources/[32] Dettmers et al. - QLoRA - Efficient finetuning of quantized LLMs.pdf` (no alternate version file exists in the repo)
- **Publication status:** 37th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2023) (header, p. 1 of the PDF).
- **Problem & context:** Standard 16-bit finetuning of a 65B-parameter LLaMA model needs more than 780GB of GPU memory, and existing quantized-model approaches work only for inference and break during training, limiting who can finetune large models and how broadly finetuning behaviour can be studied.
- **Approach:** QLoRA backpropagates gradients through a frozen, 4-bit quantized language model into Low-Rank Adapters. Its three components are 4-bit NormalFloat (NF4), a data type the authors describe as information-theoretically optimal for normally distributed weights; Double Quantization, which quantizes the quantization constants and saves about 0.37 bits per parameter (roughly 3GB on a 65B model); and Paged Optimizers to survive memory spikes (Abstract, Sections 2 and 4). The study finetunes over 1,000 models across 8 instruction datasets, LLaMA and T5 architectures, and scales up to 65B, producing the Guanaco chat models from an OASST1-derived dataset.
- **Key findings:** QLoRA finetunes a 65B model on a single 48GB GPU without degrading task performance relative to 16-bit finetuning (Abstract). Guanaco 65B reaches 99.3% of ChatGPT's performance level on the Vicuna benchmark after 24 hours of finetuning on one GPU (Abstract, Section 5.2). Data quality dominates quantity: a 9k-sample dataset (OASST1) outperformed a 450k-sample FLAN v2 subset (data analysis). GPT-4-based evaluation correlates imperfectly with human judgement, with Kendall tau of 0.43 (evaluation section), and the paper warns current chatbot benchmarks are unreliable (Abstract).
- **Cited in the report at:** Chapter 2: Literature Review > 2.2 Quantization Methods and Capability Effects

## [25] LLM.int8(): 8-bit Matrix Multiplication for Transformers at Scale
- **Bibliography entry:** [25] T. Dettmers, M. Lewis, Y. Belkada, and L. Zettlemoyer, “LLM.int8(): 8-bit matrix multiplication for transformers at scale,” in Proc. NeurIPS, 2022. arXiv:2208.07339.
- **Repo PDF:** `Sources/[33] Dettmers et al. - LLM.int8() - 8-bit matrix multiplication for transformers at scale.pdf` (no alternate version file exists in the repo)
- **Publication status:** 36th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2022) (header, p. 1 of the PDF).
- **Problem & context:** Transformer language models develop highly systematic emergent outlier features at around 6.7B parameters that dominate attention and predictive performance and destroy accuracy under conventional 8-bit quantization, so prior Int8 methods were restricted to models below 350M parameters or degraded quality at scale.
- **Approach:** LLM.int8() applies Int8 matrix multiplication to the feed-forward and attention projection layers using two components: vector-wise quantization with separate normalization constants for each inner product, and a mixed-precision decomposition that computes the small set of outlier feature dimensions (about 0.1%) in 16-bit while more than 99.9% of values remain in 8-bit (Abstract, Section 4). The method converts checkpoints at load time with no quantization-specific tuning and is evaluated on OPT, GPT-3-class, and BLOOM models from 125M up to 175B parameters, with C4/WikiText perplexity and zero-shot accuracy comparisons against absmax, row-wise, and vector-wise baselines.
- **Key findings:** Inference memory is cut by half with no performance degradation up to 175B parameters, and the BLOOM-176B memory footprint falls by 1.96x (Abstract, Section 4). At 13B parameters on C4, LLM.int8() retains the 16-bit baseline perplexity of 12.45 while standard 8-bit methods fail beyond the 6.7B scale (perplexity table, Section 4). Analysis shows about 150,000 outliers concentrated in at most 7 feature dimensions at the 6.7B scale; zeroing them reduces top-1 attention softmax probability from about 40% to 20% and raises perplexity by 600-1000% (outlier analysis, Section 5).
- **Cited in the report at:** Chapter 2: Literature Review > 2.2 Quantization Methods and Capability Effects

## [32] OmniQuant: Omnidirectionally Calibrated Quantization for Large Language Models
- **Bibliography entry:** [32] W. Shao et al., “OmniQuant: Omnidirectionally calibrated quantization for large language models,” in Proc. International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR), 2024.
- **Repo PDF:** `Sources/[38] Shao et al. - OmniQuant - Omnidirectionally calibrated quantization for large language models.pdf` (no alternate version file exists in the repo)
- **Publication status:** Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2024 (header, p. 1 of the PDF).
- **Problem & context:** Existing post-training quantization for LLMs hand-crafts their quantization parameters, which limits accuracy especially at extreme low-bit settings such as W4A4 and W2A16, while quantization-aware training alternatives need far more data and compute. OmniQuant aims to keep PTQ cheap while reaching QAT-like quality across many quantization configurations.
- **Approach:** OmniQuant keeps full-precision weights frozen and makes the quantization process itself learnable through block-wise minimization of output error, using two components: Learnable Weight Clipping (LWC) to modulate extreme weight values via learned clipping thresholds, and Learnable Equivalent Transformation (LET), which shifts quantization difficulty from activations to weights in a generalization of SmoothQuant/AWQ-style equivalent transformations (Abstract, Section 3). Calibration uses only 128 samples on a single A100-40G GPU, taking from 1 to 16 hours depending on model size (Section 1); the PDF-extracted abstract renders this range as "116 hours", an apparent text-extraction flattening of the range. Settings span W4A16, W3A16, W2A16, W6A6, and W4A4 across OPT, LLaMA, and LLaMA-2 families up to 70B, plus instruction-tuned models.
- **Key findings:** At W4A4 on LLaMA-7B, OmniQuant reaches 52.65% average accuracy versus 38.41% for SmoothQuant and 46.43% for LLM-QAT (Figure 1a and results tables), improving average accuracy by +4.99 to +11.80 percentage points across models at W4A4 (Section 4.3). At W2A16 on LLaMA-13B it attains WikiText2 perplexity 13.21 while GPTQ's increases sharply (reported as 3832 in Section 1/Figure 1b). It also improves win rates for quantized instruct models and nearly doubles real-device inference speed at W4A16g128 via MLC-LLM deployment (instruction-tuned and device sections).
- **Cited in the report at:** Chapter 2: Literature Review > 2.3 Quantization and Safety Behaviour
