The Whole Statistics Story
in Twelve Steps

A lazy-friendly condensation of the full interactive explainer. Read top to bottom once and you will understand every number in Chapter 4 of your report.

⏱️ ≈ 5 minutes #️⃣ every number = real artifact 🔗 deep-dive links included

  1. 🧬 Two copies of the same brain

    Each of the five “pairs” is one model loaded twice: normally (fp16) and quantized to 4-bit (NF4). Both copies answer the identical prompts, in order, greedily, seed 42. Because everything except precision is held constant, any behaviour change you measure can be blamed on quantization rather than on different prompts or different checkpoints.

    In your report: §3.1 calls this the matched-pair design; revision pinning keeps “same model” literally true.

    Deep dive: matched pairs →

  2. 🏷️ Every answer becomes a yes/no label

    HarmBench asks 200 unsafe questions (complied? yes/no), XSTest 250 benign ones (refused? yes/no), MMLU 300 and ARC-Challenge 1172 multiple-choice items (correct? yes/no). All four headline metrics — ASR, over-refusal rate, MMLU accuracy, ARC accuracy — are just yes-count ÷ total, e.g. 20/200 = 0.100.

    In your report: Table 3.2 lists the sizes; Mistral’s baseline ASR 0.585 literally means 117 of 200 compliances.

    Deep dive: rates and samples →

  3. ➖ Subtract: the delta is the star

    Δ = NF4 rate − fp16 rate. It answers “how big is the change?” before anyone asks whether the change is real. Sign rules: positive ΔASR/ΔOR = worse (more compliance/refusal); negative capability Δ = worse (accuracy lost).

    In your report: every “Δ…” column of Tables 4.1–4.2; e.g. Llama ΔASR = 0.060 − 0.100 = −0.040.

    Deep dive: deltas →

  4. 🎲 One number always wobbles

    A different batch of prompts would give a slightly different Δ even if the model truly didn’t change. Small samples wobble more — which is why ARC’s n=1172 can resolve a shift of 0.016 while HarmBench’s n=200 struggles to resolve 0.04.

    In your report: §3.3 says unequal benchmark sizes “yield different detection thresholds.”

    Deep dive: the wobble widget →

  5. 🥞 The bootstrap: fake reruns from your own data

    To learn how much Δ would wobble without paying for more GPU time, resample your own prompt-pairs with replacement (same pair picked twice is allowed; both arms always travel together), recompute Δ, repeat 10,000 times, and take the middle 95%. That range is the 95% confidence interval. If zero is inside it, “no change” is still a live explanation.

    In your report: Qwen3-1.7B MMLU: Δ = −0.090, CI [−0.140, −0.043] — zero safely outside.

    Deep dive: bootstrap lab (clickable!) →

  6. ⚔️ McNemar: judge only the disagreements

    Prompts both models answer the same way say nothing about change. Among prompts they disagree on, assume “no real effect” makes each disagreement a fair coin flip. If the actual up/down split is rarer than luck allows, that’s a small p-value. Hand-check for Llama: 1 vs 9 splits out of 10 → p = (1+10)/1024 × 2 = 0.0215.

    In your report: Qwen3-1.7B flipped 16 prompts up and 16 down yet ΔASR = 0.000 — flat overall can still hide churn (§4.2).

    Deep dive: McNemar calculator →

  7. 🎰 Twenty tests = twenty ways to be fooled

    The study runs 5 pairs × 4 outcomes = 20 hypothesis tests. Even in a world where nothing is true, the “p<0.05” rule rings false-positively about once per experiment (and ~64% of experiments ring at least once).

    In your report: §3.5 declares the family of 20 primary contrasts up front, so correction is principled, not cherry-picked.

    Deep dive: false-positive factory →

  8. 🎯 Benjamini–Hochberg: the honesty staircase

    Sort the 20 p-values smallest-first. Rank k must beat threshold (k/20)×0.05 — a rising staircase. Find the highest rank that clears its step; everyone up to it survives, and each p becomes an adjusted q-value. Result here: exactly 3 survivors — Qwen-1.7B MMLU −0.090 (q=0.008), Llama ARC −0.032 (q=0.008), Phi over-refusal −0.048 (q=0.012) — and none of them is harmful compliance. That IS the report’s headline: no ASR increase survives correction.

    In your report: Tables 4.1/4.2 “p / q” columns; §4.1’s “exactly three”; the abstract’s central claim.

    Deep dive: interactive BH explorer with your real 20 p-values →

  9. 🔍 Who graded the grader?

    Every Δ flows through a scorer, so scorers get audited too. Raw % agreement flatters lazy raters when positives are rare (regex vs classifier: 65% agreement but κ = 0.36). Against human labels: HarmBench classifier κ = 0.59 vs regex 0.11; XSTest semantic judge κ = 0.485 vs regex −0.006 (the regex caught 2 of 63 true refusals). This is why Phi’s surviving over-refusal decrease is called a scorer artifact: statistics passed it, measurement validation failed it.

    In your report: §4.3’s “scorer-dependent”, §4.7 RQ5, abstract’s last sentences.

    Deep dive: kappa playground →

  10. 🔋 Could this study even see small effects?

    “Not significant” can mean “too small to detect,” so the project computes a detection floor: at HarmBench scale (n=200, typical disagreement rate 0.09) only ASR shifts of about ±0.06+ are reliably catchable — and every observed NF4 ASR delta sits below that. So non-significant ASR rows are “unresolved,” not “proven safe.” On capability, exactly two effects clear their floors (Qwen-MMLU −0.090 vs floor 0.072; Llama ARC −0.032 vs floor 0.026).

    In your report: §4.4 “unresolved rather than claims of equivalence”; the power note in multiple_comparisons.json.

    Deep dive: MDE calculator →

  11. ⚖️ “Not different” isn’t “the same”: enter TOST

    Failing to find a difference never proves closeness. To claim INT8 keeps capability intact, the report flips the question: can we rule out being worse than −0.05 AND better than +0.05? Passing both one-sided tests (equivalently: the 90% CI fits inside the ±0.05 band) licenses a genuine equivalence claim. Verdict: all 10 INT8 capability contrasts pass at the pre-declared ±0.05 margin.

    In your report: §4.6 “practical similarity… within the stated ±0.05 margin” and the staged deployment advice.

    Deep dive: TOST picture →

  12. 🛡️ Finally, the conclusion gets beaten up on purpose

    Sensitivity checks ask whether the story survives changed conditions: five decoding seeds per pair (greedy deltas mostly sit inside the seed spread); generation budget (the scary 128-token era was 60.3% truncated responses — the artefact evaporates at 512); alternative judges (gpt-4o κ 0.68–0.95 and open-weight LlamaGuard κ 0.36–0.92 both find no significant ASR increase either). Nothing overturns the primary findings — which is what lets them be stated plainly.

    In your report: §4.7 RQ5 synthesis; §4.6’s INT8 anomaly resolution.

    Deep dive: robustness toolkit →

📇 The jargon cheat-sheet

The outcomes

ASRshare of unsafe prompts the model complies with (higher = worse)
Over-refusal (OR)share of benign prompts refused (higher = worse)
MMLU / ARC accuracyshare answered correctly (lower = worse)
Δ (delta)NF4 minus fp16 — the effect size

Measuring uncertainty

Bootstrap CIrange of Δs produced by resampling your own pairs 10,000×; middle 95%
“zero inside the CI”no-change remains plausible ⇒ non-significant
b and cthe two flip directions among prompts where the pair members disagree
McNemar podds that pure coin-flip luck produces a split as lopsided as observed
exact testcomputed from true binomial probabilities — no approximations to break at small counts
p-valueP(data this dramatic | nothing real happening). Not the chance your finding is true!

Deciding what counts

α = 0.05the pre-agreed surprise threshold for a single test
BH-FDR / q-valuep-value penalised because 20 tests ran together; q<0.05 = survivor
Bonferroniharsher alternative (threshold 0.0025) — rejected here for blindness
Powerchance a real effect gets detected (target 80%)
MDEsmallest effect reliably detectable — ≈ ±0.06 for ASR at n=200
TOST / ±0.05 margintwo flipped tests proving “within half a point of unchanged” (used for INT8)

Trusting the instruments

Cohen’s κagreement between two raters after deleting what blind luck would produce
Prevalence traprare positives make raw agreement look great and κ look modest
Precision / recallof my flags, share right / of real cases, share caught (XSTest regex: 0.29 / 0.03)
Human gold set200-item human-labelled sample used to referee scorer fights
Scorer sensitivityrerun the analysis under a different judge; do conclusions survive?
Floor effecta rate pinned near zero (Mistral OR 0.004) hides changes and deflates κ

🌟 If you remember only five things

  1. Size first, luck second. Δ says how big; CI/p/q say whether luck explains it. Never quote one without the other.
  2. Non-significant ≠ zero. It means “not detected” — the ±0.06 ASR detection floor is why.
  3. After correcting 20 tests, exactly 3 survive — none is a harm increase. That sentence is your report’s spine.
  4. A number is only as good as its ruler. Phi’s “significant” over-refusal drop died the moment humans checked the regex.
  5. To claim “close enough”, use TOST (that’s how INT8 earned its equivalence badge).
